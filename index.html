Who she is

Margaret W. Piccoli is a lifelong educator, an author, and an improv enthusiast. For more than 25 years she has taught languages in classrooms on two continents: high school Spanish in Louisiana, English as a world language at Università Cattolica in Italy, Italian at Ithaca College, and now world language education and English as a second language at LSU.

Her teaching and research sit where language learning meets the theatre. She studies second language teaching methods, improvisation, and how the arts help students speak, understand, and stay with a new language. She argues that improvisation considers the whole learner and gives people more than one way to express themselves. Her passion is the teaching and advancement of a multilingual society.

At LSU she is a professor of practice in the School of Education, College of Human Sciences & Education. Her 2018 dissertation examined whether theatrical improvisation could raise students’ self-efficacy, oral proficiency, and satisfaction in second language learning.
